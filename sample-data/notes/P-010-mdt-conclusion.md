# MDT conclusion – Colorectal tumour board – 10 Mar 2026

**Patient:** Sanne de Vries (P-010), 62 y
**Present:** Colorectal surgery, radiation oncology, medical oncology, radiology, pathology,
colorectal nurse specialist

**Case:** Mid-rectal adenocarcinoma, cT3c cN1 M0, mesorectal fascia free, EMVI negative. Long-course
chemoradiotherapy (50.4 Gy with capecitabine) completed 22 Dec 2025 without interruption.

**Restaging:** MRI 4 Mar 2026 shows mrTRG 2, a near-complete response with a small residual scar.
Endoscopy 5 Mar 2026 shows a flat white scar without visible tumour; biopsies negative. CEA fell
from 4.1 to 2.2 ng/mL.

**Discussion:** The response is good enough to discuss organ preservation. Two options are
considered equivalent in this situation and the choice depends on what the patient values.

1. **Watch-and-wait (organ preservation).** No operation now. Intensive follow-up: endoscopy and
   MRI every 3 months in the first two years, then less often. Regrowth occurs in roughly 1 in 4
   patients, almost always in the first two years, and is still operable when found in time.
   No stoma now, bowel function preserved.
2. **Total mesorectal excision (surgery).** Removal of the rectum with the tumour bed. Highest
   certainty about what is left behind, but a major operation with a temporary stoma in most
   patients, a risk of long-term bowel and sexual dysfunction, and 4–6 weeks of recovery.

Long-term survival is expected to be comparable in both paths; watch-and-wait requires strict
adherence to follow-up.

**Conclusion:** Offer the patient a shared decision between watch-and-wait and total mesorectal
excision. No decision taken at the board.
